Silver Lake and PIF reportedly discussed buying Unity at about $25B. No deal has been agreed, and Unity is reportedly working with a bank on its options.

After EA, this would take the same two buyers from owning games to owning the engine and ad tools that studios build and sell with.

What a buyer has to believe at $25B:

1. The price. It's about 16% above Unity's market cap of roughly $21.5B after Q3. Q3 adjusted EBITDA was $257M, a 41% margin. Annualised, $25B is about 24 times that.

2. The cash. Q3 free cash flow was $220M. On a run-rate that's about a 3.5% yield on $25B, so the case needs growth to keep going.

3. Where the growth comes from. Grow Solutions, the ads business, rose 44% to $457M. Create, the engine, went to $165M from $152M. Q4 guidance is $290M to $295M of adjusted EBITDA, which would bring the multiple down to about 21 times.

So most of the case rests on the ad network, since the engine grows slowly.

What I'll watch: whether Unity confirms a sale process.

Deal people here: at roughly 24 times EBITDA, would you underwrite it?

Sources: Reuters via PocketGamer.biz and Enterprise AM; Unity Q3 2026 results (SEC filing); tbreak
