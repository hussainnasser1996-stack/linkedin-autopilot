Saudi savers now keep more in time and savings deposits than in current accounts. Argaam reports it's the first time since 2008. The slides show why banks care.

End of September, from SAMA data:
Time and savings: SAR 1.417tn, 46.1% of deposits
Demand: SAR 1.405tn, 45.7%
A year earlier the split was 39.3% and 50.4%.

Two things I'd watch:

1. Time deposits pay interest. Current accounts mostly don't. So the same deposit base costs banks more to fund.

2. How much of that reaches margins. Asharq Al-Awsat notes a higher share doesn't have to lift funding costs one for one. Q4 results will show it.

If you work in a bank treasury here, are you seeing it in your cost of funds?

Sources: SAMA data via Asharq Al-Awsat and Argaam
