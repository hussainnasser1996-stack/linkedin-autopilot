Mubadala and Centerbridge are paying about £1.17bn for Canaccord's UK wealth business — not for its profits, but for £43.4bn of client money.

That's roughly 2.7% of client assets. It's a pivotal signal for Gulf capital abroad.

Here's what the buyers have to believe:

1. The money stays. Wealth managers get paid a fee on the assets they look after. If clients leave, the price stops making sense.

2. The fees keep growing. Revenue was a reported £261.1m in the year to March 2026, up 11.6%. At £1.17bn, that's about 4.5 times revenue.

Management also keeps a meaningful stake, which aligns incentives, ensures continuity and reduces execution risk.

The deal should close in H1 2027.

Deal people here: how would you price a wealth manager, on assets or on revenue?
