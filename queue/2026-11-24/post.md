## Caption

Mubadala and Centerbridge are paying about £1.17bn for Canaccord's UK wealth business. It looks after £43.4bn of client money.

So the price is roughly 2.7% of client assets.

What the buyers have to believe:

1. The money stays. A wealth manager earns a fee on the assets it looks after, so the price only works if clients don't leave.

2. The fees keep growing. Revenue was a reported £261.1m in the year to March 2026, up 11.6%. At £1.17bn, that's about 4.5 times revenue.

Management keeps a meaningful stake, so the people who hold the client relationships stay owners too.

The deal is expected to close in the first half of 2027.

Deal people here: how would you price a wealth manager, on assets or on revenue?

Sources: Mubadala (16 Nov 2026); The National; Family Wealth Report
