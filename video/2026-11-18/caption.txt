Ma'aden's revenue rose 11% last quarter. EBITDA fell 24%.

Phosphate is still almost half of sales ($1,370M). Its EBITDA dropped from $774M to $135M. Ma'aden points to high sulfur costs and no ammonia export sales.

Two things caught my eye:

1. Aluminum and gold now earn 86% of segment EBITDA. A year ago, phosphate alone made 64%.

2. Gold does it on 20% of revenue, at a 68% margin.

Most people still think of Ma'aden as a phosphate company. This quarter, the profit came from metals.
