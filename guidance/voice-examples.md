## Post-01 — The $860M Saudi VC signal
Everyone keeps quoting the $860M Saudi VC figure like it settles something. It doesn't.

The headline is real. But the headline is the least interesting thing about it.

What I'd actually want to know: of that capital, how much went into follow-ons versus new deals, which sectors got repeat cheques, and which rounds had regional leads versus foreign leads flying in for a day. That's the deployment texture — and it tells you what's actually underwritten in the Kingdom right now, as opposed to what got a press release.

A number like $860M is a scoreboard. Deployment pattern is a map.

Most people are reading the scoreboard and skipping the map.

## Post-12 — Dry powder isn't the Gulf's problem; discipline is
The global VC headline in 2026 is still about dry powder — who has it, who's sitting on it, who's slowly deploying.

In the Gulf the conversation is different.

Capital is available. Sovereign programs, family offices, sector-specific vehicles — there is no shortage of allocators. What's scarce is disciplined deployment: the ability to move at regional pace, respect local regulatory reality, underwrite without imported frameworks, and wait out pipelines that aren't on quarterly cycles.

Most failed deployments here weren't undercapitalised. They were undisciplined — imported pacing onto a market that doesn't move that way.

Dry powder isn't the problem. Discipline is.

## Post-17 — The real GCC healthtech bottleneck is insurance
Every month I see another GCC healthtech pitch with a brilliant clinical product and a distribution plan that assumes the reimbursement side solves itself.

It does not.

Regional healthcare spending is dominated by a small number of payer structures — government, mandatory insurance schemes, and a thin layer of private. Most clinical innovations hit one or more of these payers and stall for eighteen months while the reimbursement code gets created, reviewed, repriced.

The technology isn't the bottleneck. The technology is ahead.

The insurance reimbursement rails are what determine whether a good clinical product becomes a viable business. Any healthtech founder here who doesn't have a concrete plan for that specific path is planning a long tunnel.

The companies that crack it first will compound for a decade.

## Post-22 — Aramco Q3 2026: the headline isn't the story
Aramco's net income jumped 35% this quarter.

Look closer. That's not really the story.

SAR 25.9B of it is a one-off gain from selling its stake in PRefChem. Strip that out and adjusted net income is up 9%. Still good. Just not 35%.

Two things caught my eye:

1. Crude averaged $95.2 a barrel, up from $70.1 a year ago. Yet Upstream EBIT fell 9%. Lower volumes and higher royalties ate the price gain.

2. Downstream did the lifting. Almost 90% of the revenue growth came from refining and chemicals.

For years the Aramco story was simple: oil up, profit up. This quarter it's more about what they do with the barrel after it leaves the ground.

I put the whole quarter into a 35-second video. Every number checked against Aramco's interim report.

## Post-23: Saudi game studios have a seed problem. Actually, an after-seed problem.
Look at the Saudi game studio rounds this year.

Fahy Studios: $1.75M
Ash Games: $1.5M
Majestic Mind: $1.45M
Floaty Studio: $1M
Khosouf: $600K

All seed. Almost all led by the same two funds, Merak and Impact46. Drake Star ranks both among the most active seed investors in MENA games over the last 12 months.

That part is working. Two government-backed gaming funds, SAR 450M between them, and a real pipeline of studios.

Now the question I keep coming back to: what happens after seed?

I couldn't find a single Saudi studio Series A this year. Meanwhile PIF owns EA, Savvy owns Scopely, and the top of the market is being bought at tens of billions.

So the money is at the very top and the very bottom. The middle looks empty.

Two things could fill it:
1. Growth rounds led by regional funds that actually understand games
2. Revenue-share financing, the way Kowloon Nights and Griffin do it globally. I haven't seen anyone in the region offer it yet.

Whoever builds that middle layer gets first look at the next Kammelna.

If you're building or backing studios here, what am I missing?
